1.1

Architecture & PoC

The overall shape of the system — how a question gets in, which AWS service does each job, and how the answer comes back.

  • Three ways to build: Amazon Q Business is ready-made, Bedrock is build-your-own, SageMaker is when you want full control of the machines.
  • Streaming the answer word by word uses AppSync over WebSocket, so the user is not staring at a blank screen.
  • Worth memorising: if your documents come from SharePoint, Salesforce or Confluence, you must use OpenSearch Serverless.

1.2

Picking & Looking After the Model

Choosing which model to use, teaching it your house style, and proving it is good enough before it goes live.

  • Decide your pass marks first, then test. Otherwise you talk yourself into whatever you tried first.
  • LoRA trains a small add-on layer and leaves the original model untouched — cheap, fast, and easy to swap out.
  • Catch for the exam: speed and cost are not part of model evaluation. Evaluation only measures quality.
  • Models quietly get worse over time as the real world changes. That is drift, and you have to watch for it.

1.3

Getting the Data Ready

Cleaning up messy input — forms, photos, phone calls — before any of it reaches the model.

  • Four different checkers, because each catches what the others miss: rules, visual exploration, custom code, and a trend alarm.
  • Anything that fails goes to a person, never to the bin.
  • Turn the temperature down (0 to 0.2) when the same question must give the same answer every time, like money decisions.
  • Change temperature or top_p, never both — change both and you cannot tell which one caused the difference.

1.4

Where the Numbers Live

Picking the right database for your vectors, making it fast at scale, and making sure one client cannot see another client’s files.

  • No single store fits everything. Public documents, confidential files and live status flags each want a different one.
  • Narrow down first, then do the maths. Filter 15 million documents to 5 by date and court, then compare vectors on just those 5.
  • Permissions are checked before results come back, not filtered out afterwards.

1.5

Finding the Right Pieces

The quality side of search: how documents are cut up, how they become numbers, and how the best few are chosen.

  • Cut on meaning, not on word count. A clause split in the wrong place can reverse what it says.
  • Titan turns each piece into 1,536 numbers. Fewer numbers is cheaper and faster but loses fine detail.
  • Use the same embedding model when saving and when searching, or nothing matches any more.
  • Search wide, then narrow. Grab about 100 candidates quickly, then let a reranker pick the best 10 carefully.

1.6

Prompts & Keeping It Legal

Treating the prompt as real production code: reviewed, versioned, tested, and provable after the fact.

  • Three layers, because one is never enough: the prompt tells the model what to do, Guardrails stop it saying things, and a JSON schema forces the answer into a fixed shape.
  • Counter-intuitive: on Guardrails, LOW is the strictest setting and HIGH is the loosest.
  • Test the new prompt against the old one on the same set of questions. If it fixes one thing and breaks another, it does not ship.
  • CloudTrail records who did it; CloudWatch records what was said. The first one is your seven-year audit trail.
